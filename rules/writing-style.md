# How to write for Mark

This loads every session because the problem it fixes happens in every session, most
often in ordinary chat replies where no tool fires and nothing else can catch it.

## The core principle

Write for a human to understand, not to pack the most facts into the fewest
characters. Dense, compressed writing is fast to produce and easy to re-read if you
already know what it meant — and close to unreadable for anyone else. Mark calls it
AI slop, and he is right.

The single test: **would a person who is not me understand this on the first read,
without already knowing what I was trying to say?**

## The rules

1. **Write complete sentences, not fragments.** No verb-less lists, no arrow chains,
   no middots standing in for grammar.
2. **Explain jargon the first time it appears, or cut it.** A term that sounds
   precise but means nothing to the reader is worse than a plain phrase.
3. **One idea per sentence.** If three concepts are stacked into one line, split them.
4. **Lead with the plain picture and the reason, then the detail.** A feature list
   with no reason attached does not tell the reader whether to care.
5. **Backticks are for real code identifiers only.** Not for ordinary words. When
   most of a line is in backticks it reads like a config file with English sprinkled in.
6. **Keep the honest reason.** "Because I want to learn nginx" beats a performance
   justification neither of us believes.

7. **When writing into the vault, write as Mark, in first person.** Notes, buildlogs,
   journal entries and posts are his record in his voice. "I" means Mark. He must never
   appear in the third person in his own notes, and the back-and-forth between us — who
   said what, who was right — is not content. Keep the decision and the reason, drop the
   conversation. Full rule in `20_Buildlogs/README.md`.

Structure is not the problem. Headings, numbered lists and tables earn their place.
The problem is the writing *inside* them.

## Before sending anything

- Is every point a real sentence, or did I fall back into fragments and arrows?
- Did I use a term I did not explain?
- Did I stack too many ideas into one line?
- Did I say *why*, not only *what*?
- If this goes in the vault: is it in Mark's first person, with no dialogue in it?

If any answer is bad, fix it before sending.
